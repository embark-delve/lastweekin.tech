CBP facility codes sure seem to have leaked via online flashcards

Ars Technica General Tech

A Quizlet flashcard set titled “USBP Review” was publicly available from February until March 20, when it was made private after WIRED flagged a phone number that could belong to a user near a Kingsville, Texas CBP facility. Tech companies are trying to neuter Colorado’s landmark right-to-repair law

Ars Technica AI

Right‑to‑repair momentum is accelerating in the U.S., with Colorado leading the charge by passing a series of bills in 2022 that grant consumers the tools, instructions, and legal rights to fix wheelchairs, farm equipment, and consumer electronics; the state’s 2024 Consumer Right to Repair Digital Electronic Equipment Act, effective January 2026, is the most expansive nationwide. However, a new Colorado Senate bill (SB26‑090) now seeks to carve out a “critical infrastructure” exemption—supported by tech giants such as Cisco and IBM—arguing that allowing broad repair access could compromise cybersecurity and that manufacturers would benefit from controlling repair processes. While repair advocates celebrate Colorado’s pioneering stance, manufacturers remain wary, citing potential revenue loss and security risks.

Netflix must refund customers for years of price hikes, Italian court rules

Ars Technica General Tech

A Rome court ruled that Netflix’s price hikes in Italy (2017, 2019, 2021, and 2024) were unlawful under the Consumer Code, which requires contracts to explain future price changes. The court found Netflix failed to provide valid justifications, invalidating the hikes and ordering refunds of up to €500 (≈$576) per customer, depending on their plan. Premium subscribers could receive about €500, standard about €250, and basic plan customers about €2 per month. Netflix has 90 days to notify millions of users of their refund rights or face a €700 daily penalty. The decision follows a lawsuit by Movimento Consumatori, which highlighted that Netflix’s 30‑day notice and cancellation option did not satisfy legal requirements. Netflix reportedly serves 5.4–8.3 million Italian users.

With One Million Displaced, Lebanon Turns to Digital Wallets for Aid

WIRED AI

Since March, Israeli attacks on Beirut and the occupation of southern Lebanon have displaced over a million people, many of whom have fled to Syria or are sheltering in overcrowded homes, cars, and the open air, straining Lebanon’s already fragile infrastructure. The humanitarian crisis has spurred a surge in international financial flows that bypass traditional aid channels, instead moving instantly through digital fintech platforms such as Whish Money, PayPal, Zelle, and Venmo to trusted individuals on the ground. While Lebanon receives roughly $6‑$7 billion in annual remittances—about a third of its GDP—remittance costs are high (≈11 %) and the pandemic‑era crisis has accelerated peer‑to‑peer, wallet‑based transfers, now accounting for around 70 % of inflows during the crisis. Whish Money, originally a gift‑card digitizer, has evolved into a broad financial ecosystem with 2 million users in 110 countries, targeting the unbanked and underbanked amid Lebanon’s banking collapse; its US‑bank connectivity lets diaspora funds enter Lebanese wallets instantly. This shift has enabled families to buy larger, essential purchases and stockpile supplies as uncertainty mounts, illustrating how fintech is reshaping aid delivery and daily life in a war‑torn economy.

The Ridiculously Nerdy Intel Bet That Could Rake in Billions

WIRED General Tech

Intel’s Rio Rancho Fab 9, re‑activated in January 2024 with a $500 million CHIPS‑Act grant, is now a linchpin for the company’s fast‑growing advanced‑chip‑packaging business, which combines multiple chiplets into single custom chips. The packaging arm—part of Intel’s newly split Foundry division—is positioned to compete with TSMC for AI‑driven demand, with CEO Lip‑Bu Tan calling it a key differentiator and CFO Dave Zinsner projecting packaging revenue to exceed $1 billion and even precede wafer revenue. Intel is reportedly in talks with major customers such as Google and Amazon to outsource parts of their custom chip fabrication, a move that could secure multi‑billion‑dollar deals and help the chipmaker regain market share after years of stagnation. The success of this Foundry strategy hinges on attracting such external clients while maintaining high gross margins comparable to its traditional product line.
